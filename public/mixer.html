Glossary

Reference

MeterLab’s stats are quick “signals” about a track. They’re best used for comparison and decision-making (e.g., matching a playlist, checking mix balance), not as absolute truth.

Musical

Key

What it is: The most likely tonic (C, C#, D…).

Why it matters: Helpful for harmonic mixing, mashups, and choosing compatible samples.

Genre notes: EDM/house/DJ sets often benefit from key-matching; jazz and some live recordings may modulate and confuse single-key estimation.

Scale (Major/Minor + Modes)

What it is: A best-fit scale quality (major/minor) and common modes (dorian, phrygian, lydian, mixolydian, locrian).

Why it matters: Gives a hint of “mood” (bright vs dark) and common chord vocabulary.

Genre notes: Rock/blues often leans mixolydian; metal can lean phrygian; funk can show dorian; pop is frequently major/minor.

Certainty

What it is: How separated the top key/mode guess is from the runner-up guesses (a “trust meter”, not a guarantee of correctness).

Why it matters: Low certainty usually means: weak tonal center, lots of noise/percussion, modulation, or a pitch set shared by multiple modes (relative ambiguity).

Genre notes: Ambient/drone and very percussive tracks often produce lower certainty.

Tempo

BPM

What it is: Estimated tempo (beats per minute) from onset periodicity (transient pulses).

Why it matters: Useful for beatmatching, planning transitions, and picking time-synced effects.

Genre notes: EDM/house/techno typically yields strong BPM confidence; classical, rubato, and ambient may not have a stable tempo.
